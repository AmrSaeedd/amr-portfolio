Project Context & Objectives

Balancing Coastal Tourism with Ecological Fragility

The Mediterranean coast of Matrouh and the Ras El Hekma / Alam El Roum sector represents one of the Mediterranean basin's most critical economic frontiers. However, rapid real estate development risks irreversible ecological degradation, alteration of natural drainage basins, and coastal erosion.

This graduation project formulates a comprehensive adaptive planning methodology. By training unsupervised K-Means clustering algorithms on multi-temporal satellite imagery, we computed environmental fragility indices based on vegetation changes (NDVI), surface moisture (NDWI), slope stability, and flash flood paths.

The resulting master plan designates development zones that harmonize with natural topographical corridors, providing resilient infrastructure, sustainable tourism hubs, and preserved ecological sanctuaries.

Key Methodological Pillars

  • 1
    Predictive Environmental Analysis Temporal tracking of NDVI and NDWI dynamics across multiple seasons to locate vulnerable biological corridors.
  • 2
    K-Means Fragility Classification Machine learning classification grouping coastal parcels into distinct vulnerability categories.
  • 3
    Adaptive Land Allocation Multi-criteria zoning ensuring low-impact densities in fragile zones and transit-oriented development in stable corridors.
  • 4
    Circulation & Drainage Network Hierarchy of roads, flood-safe evacuation routes, and dedicated surface storm runoff channels.
